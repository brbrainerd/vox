# Deep Research Systems: A From-First-Principles Technical Reference

**Date:** 2026-08-01
**Scope:** What "deep research" / agentic-research systems are architecturally, independent of any vendor product, with each concept mapped onto Vox's current (partially stubbed) pipeline. This document complements the product-specific competitive audit and code-verification pass produced alongside it. Browse **Architecture SSOTs** in the Starlight sidebar, or start at [contributor-hub](../contributors/contributor-hub.md).

---

Deep research systems are LLM-driven pipelines that turn an open-ended question into a cited, evidence-grounded report through iterated retrieval, verification, and synthesis — rather than a single-shot RAG answer. As of 2026 the field has converged on a recognizable reference architecture: **planner → multi-hop hybrid retriever → reflection/correction loop → claim verifier → novelty/redundancy filter → confidence gate → grounded synthesizer**, evaluated against benchmarks like BrowseComp-Plus and DeepResearch Bench. This document explains each stage from first principles and maps it onto Vox's existing pipeline in `vox-research-shim`, `vox-research-events`, `vox-search`, `vox-scientia`, and `vox-publisher`.

## 1. Query Planning & Decomposition

**What it is and why it matters.** A single embedding query cannot cover a multi-faceted research question — "compare X and Y across dimensions A, B, C" needs several retrieval passes, each targeting a narrower sub-question. Decomposition improves recall (each sub-query hits a tighter region of the corpus) and lets downstream stages track *which* claims answer *which* part of the original ask. 2026 surveys describe deep research systems as composed of distinct planner, query, retriever, and writer roles, with the planner handling task decomposition and subgoal scheduling ([Deep Research: A Survey of Autonomous Research Agents](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.12752v1)). Products differ mainly in how much decomposition is exposed to the user: some (e.g. Gemini Deep Research) surface the full plan for edit-before-execute, trading latency for alignment ([Zylos: Deep Research Agent Architectures](https://zylos.ai/research/2026-04-21-deep-research-agent-architectures)).

**How it's implemented.** The dominant pattern is an LLM call with a constrained output schema (`{"subqueries": [...]}`), temperature near 0, capped at a small N (typically 3–8) to bound fan-out cost, followed by deduplication and truncation. Recent RL-trained planners (DeepPlanner) treat planning as a separately optimized skill with advantage-shaped rewards over multi-turn rollouts rather than a fixed prompt, since planning quality dominates downstream benchmark scores ([DeepPlanner](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.12979)).

**Vox mapping — has it.** `crates/vox-research-shim/src/research/planner.rs::decompose_query_with_config` does exactly this: an LLM cascade call constrained to JSON schema `{"subqueries": [...]}`, clamped to `max_subqueries.clamp(1, 12)` (default 6), with case-insensitive dedup and a `passthrough_plan` fallback (single-subquery plan, flagged `planner_degraded: true`) when the LLM call or JSON parse fails. This is a solid, production-shaped implementation of the pattern — not a stub, unlike several downstream stages.

## 2. Multi-Hop Hybrid Retrieval

**What it is and why it matters.** Sparse lexical retrieval (BM25) is precise for exact terms, IDs, and rare vocabulary but blind to paraphrase; dense embedding retrieval captures semantic similarity but can miss exact-match signals. Neither alone is sufficient — 2026 production guidance is explicit that BM25 excels at exact-match queries while dense retrieval handles conceptual queries, and that fusing them raises NDCG over either alone ([Hybrid Search: BM25, Vector & Reranking Reference 2026](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/hybrid-search-bm25-vector-reranking-reference-2026)).

**How it's implemented.** The standard pipeline queries sparse and dense indexes independently (top 50–100 each), then merges with **Reciprocal Rank Fusion**: `score(d) = Σ_lists 1/(k + rank(d))`, with `k≈60` as the standard smoothing constant so top-ranked-in-one-list documents don't dominate documents ranked well in multiple lists. RRF operates purely on rank position, sidestepping the problem that BM25 and cosine-similarity scores live on incompatible scales. On WANDS, a tuned hybrid setup reaches 0.7497 NDCG, a 7.4% lift over BM25 or dense alone, and recall@10 moves from 78% to 91% when reranking is layered on ([Building a Production-Ready Hybrid Retrieval System](https://atalupadhyay.wordpress.com/2026/06/10/building-a-production-ready-hybrid-retrieval-system-from-scratch-bm25-dense-embeddings-rrf-re-ranking/)). Re-querying ("multi-hop") is triggered when evidence quality (below-threshold fused score, contradictions, low domain diversity) signals the first pass was insufficient — this is the province of CRAG, covered next.

**Vox mapping — has it.** `crates/vox-search/src/rrf.rs::rrf_merge_line_lists` implements textbook RRF with a tunable `rrf_k` (documented as "often ~60"), merging arbitrary ranked line-lists keyed by a stable dedup key (`rrf_dedup_key`, recognizing `[chunk:]`, `[repo:]`, `[node:]`, `[tantivy:]`, `[qdrant:]` prefixes). The sparse side is `vox-search/src/lexical_tantivy.rs` (tantivy/BM25), the dense side is `vox-search/src/vector_qdrant.rs` (Qdrant) and `vox-search/src/memory_hybrid.rs`; `vox-search/src/web_dispatcher.rs` and `tavily_research.rs` add live web retrieval. Multi-hop re-querying is driven by `crates/vox-research-shim/src/research/orchestrator/web_gather.rs::gather_web_hits_for_plan`, which loops up to `policy.web_search_max_hops` rounds, calling `CragRouter::should_continue` each round.

## 3. Iterative Reflection Loops (CRAG, ReAct, Reflexion)

**What it is and why it matters.** These are three related but distinct patterns for deciding "do I have enough evidence, or should I act again?" **ReAct** interleaves reasoning traces with tool calls in a single loop (Thought → Action → Observation), letting the agent decide dynamically what to retrieve next based on what it just saw ([Agentic Loops: From ReAct to Loop Engineering](https://datasciencedojo.com/blog/agentic-loops-explained-from-react-to-loop-engineering-2026-guide/)). **CRAG (Corrective RAG)** adds a lightweight *evaluator* that scores retrieved evidence quality and triggers a single corrective action (query rewrite, web fallback, knowledge refinement) before generation — a one-shot correction, not an open-ended loop. **Reflexion** goes further: draft an answer, identify what's missing via self-critique, retrieve to fill gaps, revise, repeat — a verbal-reinforcement-learning loop that iterates until the answer is judged complete ([Reflexion Agentic RAG](https://kgptalkie.com/tutorials/generative-ai/reflexion-agentic-rag)). 2026 production guidance frames the choice as layered: "start with ReAct, add Reflexion when you need self-correction... CRAG does a single correction" ([MarsDevs: Agentic RAG 2026 Production Guide](https://www.marsdevs.com/guides/agentic-rag-2026-guide)).

**How it's implemented.** CRAG evaluators are typically a lightweight classifier or LLM-judge that buckets retrieved evidence into confident/ambiguous/incorrect and picks a corrective branch; Reflexion loops store natural-language self-reflections in an episodic buffer re-injected into the next attempt's prompt context rather than doing gradient updates. Stopping conditions combine a quality threshold with a hop budget to bound cost.

**Vox mapping — has it (CRAG); partial (Reflexion-style self-check).** `crates/vox-search/src/crag.rs::CragRouter` is a direct CRAG implementation: `should_continue(current_quality, target_quality, hops_remaining)` gates continuation, and `expand_queries_from_partial_evidence` generates refined sub-queries via two heuristics — (a) regex-extracted capitalized concept terms from top-5 snippets not already in the original query, and (b) gap-oriented follow-ups when hits show `potential_contradiction` or average score < 0.55. `expand_queries_with_llm_or_heuristic` prefers LLM-generated refinements when available, falling back to the regex heuristic. The loop is driven from `web_gather.rs` with `CRAG_STOP_QUALITY` and `policy.web_search_max_hops`. Separately, `stages.rs::run_self_verification` implements a **Chain-of-Verification (CoVE)**-style pattern (not full Reflexion): generate up to 5 yes/no verification questions from the draft answer, answer each from retrieved context only, and flag `critical_inconsistency` if more than half come back "no"/"unknown." This is single-pass self-checking, not an iterate-until-satisfied Reflexion loop with revision — a real gap relative to full Reflexion.

## 4. Claim Extraction & Verification

**What it is and why it matters.** Once evidence is gathered, individual factual assertions ("claims") need per-claim entailment classification against evidence, not just a holistic "does this look plausible" pass — this is what lets a system cite precisely and flag contradictions. The canonical taxonomy comes from FEVER/SciFact-style fact-verification: **SUPPORT / REFUTE (Contradict) / NOT ENOUGH INFO**, cast as a textual-entailment (NLI) task over (claim, evidence) pairs ([SciClaims](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.18526); [Scientific Claim Verification with Fine-Tuned NLI Models](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2024/129000/129000.pdf)).

**How it's implemented.** Production systems increasingly use small, distilled, calibrated fact-checkers rather than full LLM judges for cost — **MiniCheck** and its descendants (Bespoke-MiniCheck-7B, FactCG, VeriFastScore) match GPT-4-level faithfulness scoring at a fraction of the cost by fine-tuning compact NLI models on synthetic claim-evidence pairs. Systems increasingly go beyond a single scalar to typed error taxonomies.

**Vox mapping — has it, with a documented taxonomy gap.** `crates/vox-research-shim/src/research/verifier.rs` implements `verify_claims_with_config`: for each claim, an LLM cascade call (temperature 0, JSON-schema-constrained) classifies against up to 8 evidence snippets, producing a `Verdict` (`Supported | Contradicted | Contested | Unverified`) with confidence, an `abstain_threshold` (default 0.5), and per-span `EvidenceSpan`s typed `Supporting | Contradicting | Background`. The file's own doc comment flags that this taxonomy is a legacy compatibility shim, not the canonical one: the SCIENTIA plan specifies SciFact's `Support/Contradict/NotEnoughInfo/Abstain`, and reconciling to it is explicitly deferred to "Phase 1's `vox-claim-extractor` integration." `VerifierConfig.nli_model_id` is a placeholder wired to a `FALLBACK_NLI_MODEL_ID` registry constant — Vox's design already anticipates swapping the current LLM-judge verifier for a dedicated NLI/MiniCheck-style model, but that swap hasn't landed. `crates/vox-scientia/` is the crate intended to own claim extraction proper.

## 5. Novelty / Redundancy / Information-Gain Scoring

**What it is and why it matters.** Without dedup, an agent burns its hop/token budget re-confirming the same fact from restated sources, and a final report reads as padded rather than comprehensive. Deduplication needs to operate at multiple granularities: exact/near-exact text (shingling/MinHash), semantic near-duplicates (embedding similarity), and — for research-worthiness specifically — novelty relative to *prior published work*, not just relative to this session's evidence.

**How it's implemented.** The standard toolkit layers: exact hash dedup → MinHash/LSH on n-gram shingles with Jaccard-similarity thresholds (~75% typical) for near-duplicates → embedding-based semantic dedup via an ANN index for paraphrase-level redundancy ([MinHash LSH in Milvus](https://milvus.io/blog/minhash-lsh-in-milvus-the-secret-weapon-for-fighting-duplicates-in-llm-training-data.md)). "Information gain" gating means scoring a new candidate fact by how much *new* shingle/embedding mass it contributes versus content already accepted, not merely whether it's a binary duplicate.

**Vox mapping — has two independent implementations at different maturity, not yet unified.** (1) `crates/vox-search/src/novelty.rs::NoveltyScorer` — a lightweight per-session scorer: 4-gram character shingles hashed with FNV-1a into a `HashSet`; `score()` returns the fraction of a new hit's shingles not already seen, and `accept()` commits them. Jaccard-adjacent but simpler than true MinHash (no LSH bucketing), fine at session scale. (2) `crates/vox-publisher/src/scientia_prior_art.rs` + `crates/vox-research-events/src/schema_types.rs::NoveltyEvidenceBundle` — a considerably more sophisticated **cross-corpus novelty pipeline**: federated queries against OpenAlex, Crossref, and Semantic Scholar, producing `NormalizedHit`s with both `lexical_score` and `semantic_score`, bucketed `NoveltyRecencyBucket` (VeryRecent/Recent/Stale), and an `OverlapSummary` — used to assess whether a research *finding* is prior-art-novel before self-publication, not to dedup retrieval hits mid-session. Separately, `crates/vox-similarity/` (`signature.rs`, `index.rs`) implements genuine MinHash signatures, currently consumed by `vox-skill-discovery` and `vox-orchestrator`'s similarity services, not yet by `vox-search`'s per-session novelty scorer — a natural, low-risk upgrade path if `novelty.rs`'s FNV shingle-set approach needs LSH-scale performance.

## 6. Cross-Encoder Reranking

**What it is and why it matters.** RRF fusion combines *independently computed* rank signals — it never lets the query and document interact except through separate encoders. A cross-encoder jointly encodes `(query, document)` as one sequence and outputs a single relevance score, capturing token-level interactions bi-encoders and BM25 both miss. This is why the 2026 consensus production stack is explicitly "BM25 + dense + RRF + cross-encoder re-ranking," not RRF alone — the fused top-k (typically top 50) is re-scored by a cross-encoder before truncation to the final context window ([Hybrid Search and Re-ranking in Production RAG 2026](https://appscale.blog/en/blog/hybrid-search-and-reranking-production-rag-bm25-dense-cross-encoder-2026)).

**How it's implemented.** A small transformer (e.g., a BGE-reranker or Cohere Rerank-class model) scores each of the top-N fused candidates against the query in one forward pass per pair; too expensive over the full corpus, cheap enough over top-50.

**Vox mapping — lacking.** A repo-wide search for reranking/cross-encoder logic in `vox-search` returns no matches. Vox's retrieval pipeline stops at RRF-fused hits; there is no second-stage cross-encoder pass before hits reach the CRAG loop, verifier, or synthesizer. This is one of the more concrete, well-scoped gaps relative to 2026 production practice.

## 7. Synthesis & Citation Grounding

**What it is and why it matters.** The final report must attribute every material claim to a real, checkable source — LLMs left unconstrained will fabricate citations. 2026 work decomposes "citation hallucination" into distinct failure modes needing distinct fixes: factual, grounding, citation (existence/relevance/temporality), and reasoning errors ([Citation Grounding: Detecting and Reducing LLM Citation Hallucinations](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00898)). Reported citation-hallucination rates in production legal-domain systems run 13–21% even with grounding discipline.

**How it's implemented.** Mitigations layer: (a) grounding-discipline system prompts instructing "cite only from provided evidence, using these exact markers"; (b) structured citation schemas enforced at the output-parsing layer; (c) runtime registry checks that the cited URL/DOI/chunk-id actually resolves and exists in the evidence set; (d) sampled semantic-entailment checks on `(claim, cited passage)` pairs, reusing the NLI verifier from §4 as a post-hoc citation auditor.

**Vox mapping — has it, template fallback included; missing post-hoc audit.** `crates/vox-research-shim/src/research/orchestrator/stages.rs::synthesize_answer_with_llm` builds numbered evidence blocks (`[1] title / URL / snippet`) and instructs the synthesis LLM to cite inline matching evidence numbers — structural grounding-by-construction. It appends claim-verdict summaries from §4's verifier into the synthesis context, so contradicted/unverified claims are visible to the writer model. A deterministic `synthesize_answer_template` fallback (no network call) emits a "Verification Status" section with per-claim verdict icons and a mechanically-built "Citations" section — sidesteps hallucination entirely for the no-LLM path. What's missing relative to 2026 SOTA: no post-hoc registry check that a cited `[N]` marker in the *LLM-generated* text actually maps to real evidence, and no sampled semantic-entailment re-check between synthesized sentences and their cited spans — the verifier runs on planner-extracted claims, not on the synthesized prose itself.

## 8. Confidence / Quality Gating

**What it is and why it matters.** A single scalar like "citation count" is a weak proxy for report quality. Production systems fuse multiple signals (retrieval confidence, claim-support ratio, source diversity, contradiction rate) and increasingly build in explicit *abstention*: refuse or downgrade rather than answer confidently on thin evidence ([Engineering the RAG Stack](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.05264)). 2026 work frames multi-signal fusion as outperforming single-threshold gating for hallucination reduction, with some approaches requiring multiple independent signal families to *agree* (conjunctive gating) before granting confidence ([CalVerT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.21777)).

**How it's implemented.** A gate combines: retrieval-hit presence (hard zero-out if no hits), claim-support ratio from the verifier, source/domain diversity count, and sometimes calibrated verbal-confidence self-reports — fused into a score that routes to different response tiers.

**Vox mapping — has a documented stub in the main loop, plus a more mature parallel implementation elsewhere.** `crates/vox-research-shim/src/research/gate.rs` is explicitly self-labeled `Phase 0a STUB`: `score_with_config` fuses `citation_score` (0.35 weight), `claim_support_score` (0.30, from the verifier's `supported_claim_count`), `distinct_domain_count`-based `diversity_score` (0.20), and a hardcoded `retrieval_score = 1.0` (0.15) — already a multi-signal weighted fusion, not literally pure citation count, but the module comment concedes it's a placeholder pending fusion of contradiction ratio (notably absent from the current formula despite the verifier producing `contradicting_count` per claim). `no_retrieval_hits` triggers a hard zero (abstention-shaped), and `ConfidenceSignal::routing_tier_for` maps the score to `Direct/Light/DeepResearch` tiers — exactly the "escalate to deeper research on low confidence" pattern from the literature. Separately and more maturely, `crates/vox-research-events/src/schema_types.rs::WorthinessSignalsV2` implements genuine **conjunctive multi-signal gating** for research-finding publication: `hard_gate`/`soft_gate`/`diagnostic` signal buckets, each item carrying `passed: bool`, `score: f32`, and a `reason_code` string plus `next_actions` — closer to the CalVerT-style auditable, reason-coded gating pattern than `gate.rs` is, but it currently gates *publication worthiness*, not the retrieval-confidence routing decision in the main research loop. Unifying these two gates (or porting `WorthinessSignalsV2`'s reason-coded structure into `gate.rs`) is a concrete, low-risk architectural improvement visible directly from the code.

## 9. Evaluation Harnesses for Deep Research Systems

**What it is and why it matters.** Deep research is hard to eval because retrieval quality and reasoning/synthesis quality are confounded. 2026's two leading benchmarks disentangle these differently: **BrowseComp-Plus** (ACL 2026) fixes retrieval to a ~100K-document human-verified corpus rather than the live web, isolating agent reasoning/query-formulation quality from search-engine variance and letting retrievers be swapped to isolate their contribution. Reported numbers: with BM25 retrieval, Search-R1 gets 3.86% accuracy vs. GPT-5's 55.9%; pairing GPT-5 with a Qwen3-Embedding-8B retriever pushes accuracy to 70.1% — retriever quality alone swings accuracy ~15 points even with a fixed strong LLM ([BrowseComp-Plus](https://github.com/texttron/BrowseComp-Plus); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1023/)). **DeepResearch Bench** (100 PhD-level tasks across 22 fields, expert-authored) evaluates the *report* end-to-end — DeepResearch Bench II (Feb 2026) scores generated reports against 9,430 fine-grained binary rubrics spanning information recall, analysis quality, and presentation ([DeepResearch Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11763); [GitHub](https://github.com/Ayanami0730/deep_research_bench)). A complementary framing question is asked directly in ["How Far Are We from Genuinely Useful Deep Research Agents?"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.01948).

**Vox mapping — lacking.** `crates/vox-search/src/evaluation.rs` exists but its scope wasn't confirmed against these benchmark shapes in this pass. Nothing in the reviewed crates resembles a BrowseComp-style fixed-corpus retriever-isolation harness or a DeepResearch-Bench-style rubric-based report scorer. Vox's `gate.rs`/`verifier.rs` self-tests check unit-level correctness, but there's no end-to-end benchmark verifying the *composed* pipeline (planner → CRAG → verifier → gate → synthesis) produces reports that score well on recall/analysis/presentation rubrics, nor a fixed-corpus mode for isolating retriever contribution from LLM reasoning contribution. Building even a small internal rubric-based harness (borrowing DeepResearch Bench's binary-rubric methodology against a handful of Vox-specific research tasks) is a natural next step given the pipeline is otherwise fairly mature.
